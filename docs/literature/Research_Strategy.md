# Research strategy — Version B first (proposal-aligned)

**Primary scope:** Vedaang Chopra, *Adaptive Looped VLM — Research Vision & Implementation Blueprint* (November 22, 2026), preserved verbatim as `Project_Proposal_Reference.txt`. This is a literature-based critique and refinement, **not** a claim that the proposed experiments have run. The November 24 backbone gate and December 3 course deliverable are targets in that proposal; their completion has not been verified here.

## Decision in one sentence

Build **Version B** first: on `thinkingmachines/TML-d24-varloop-research`, freeze the entire pretrained model, use an **existing parameter-free text-guided selector** (plus resolution as an alternative), measure the full 3 visual-budget × 5 loop-pass grid, then train only a cheap *pre-inference* controller over the resulting **15-action full reward table**. Evaluate whether extra shared-decoder passes trade against visual tokens by item type and whether a controller beats fixed/heuristic allocations at matched **realized** cost. This is chiefly an empirical contribution with a modest controller, not a new vision encoder or a novel RL algorithm. *LoopZip*, *StepPrune*, *TPRL*, *GRIP-VLM* and external *SmartVL* narrow the claim (see `Research_Gap_Analysis.md`).

## 0. Feasibility gate — do not train until passed

1. **Primary backbone is varloop, fallback is gated.** On the proposed 8×A40 (48 GB) cluster, actually test whether `thinkingmachines/TML-d24-varloop-research` loads across two A40s (including any required NVFP4/MXFP8 dequantization), generates sensible responses at R=3, changes outputs at R=1–5, and performs nontrivial ScreenSpot-v2 grounding. Read the model implementation for loop control, pass-specific KV handling, projector insertion, image preprocessing, and whether a single high-R run can yield *equivalent* low-R early readouts. The supplied card's ~20 GB native checkpoint/≥32 GB inference claim is **not** an A40 BF16 compatibility guarantee. Report benchmark smoke-test results before asserting a 15-cell evaluation schedule. If this fails, follow the proposal's scoped fallback, explicitly separating token-only and loop-only studies; do not imply that a text-only Ouro backbone tests multimodal substitution.
2. **Measure R and K before learning anything.** Freeze the model and selector, test k={25%,50%,100%} × R={1,2,3,4,5} with identical examples/prompt/decoding, count realized visual tokens, and record answer, vision cost, selector overhead, each loop's prefill/decode, p50/p95 latency, memory and FLOPs. Check k=100% reproduces dense behavior; verify the requested R truly executes shared decoder blocks rather than merely changing a config label (*LoopZip* PDF §3 pp.2–5; *RecursiveVLM* PDF §3 pp.3–5). Do not make **monotonic accuracy** a loading gate: the proposal's own cited model-card results vary non-monotonically across tasks; a GQA-only monotonicity check would wrongly reject a usable checkpoint.
3. **Version B trains from cached features and complete outcome tables.** It requires neither backpropagation through the 26.4B-parameter decoder nor LoRA/GRPO. Keep controller features independent of outcome labels. If later Version C trains a differentiable vision adapter through the frozen decoder, ensure gradients reach the adapter without placing the decoder forward under `no_grad`; assert decoder weights remain unchanged (*InstructBLIP* PDF §2.6 pp.5–6; *StepPrune* PDF §3.4 pp.5–6). January decoder LoRA is a **separate, non-frozen** model condition, not evidence for the frozen-backbone result.

## 1. Version B: fixed selector, measured grid, full-information controller

- **Freeze the selector before the test grid.** Compare post-projector text-similarity, decoder-prelude attention (if available), a fixed random/uniform control, and resolution scaling on a disjoint 200-item development set; choose a primary post-encoder selector using *only* dev data. Do not assume raw cosine between independently embedded question and visual vectors is meaningful until checked; model text/vision embeddings must share a calibrated space. A prelude-attention selector incurs a decoder call whose actual cost must be included. For k=100%, require exact identity against the unmodified model. *SparseVLM* PDF §3 pp.3–5, *CRISP* PDF §III pp.2–3, *AdaptMerge* PDF §§3–4 pp.3–8, and *LoopZip* PDF pp.2–8 are direct controls.
- **Collect the 15 outcomes per training item.** Use `k∈{0.25,0.50,1.00}` and `R∈{1,2,3,4,5}`. A policy input can use question features, thumbnail features, question length, and image size **available before the decision**. It chooses one `(k,R)` pair before invoking the main decoder; it does not see intermediate loop states. If both resolution and pruning mechanisms are evaluated, keep separate grids or make mechanism an explicit action rather than silently mixing them. Count the thumbnail-controller overhead and any second vision encoding.
- **Train only a controller MLP from the complete table.** For every training example/action record reward `r_i(a)=correct_i(a)−λ·C_i(a)/C_i(full)` (or use a measured-latency variant), then optimize `−mean_i Σ_a softmax(logits_i)_a r_i(a)` with optional entropy regularization. This is **full-information cost-sensitive learning**, not a bandit, PPO or GRPO: all 15 outcomes are observed. Compare with supervised imitation of cheapest correct action, k-only, R-only, independent k/R controllers, and fixed/heuristic policies. Report deterministic argmax performance on a held-out item split at matched *realized* mean cost. The proposal supplies this objective; *Matryoshka* PDF §3 pp.4–9, *StepPrune* PDF §3 pp.3–6 and *SmartVL* [external §3.3, `Research_Gap_Analysis.md`] are related methodology, not evidence the new controller already works.
- **Define the oracle correctly.** The cheapest correct action per item is a useful diagnostic label, but its aggregated cost/accuracy is **not automatically an upper bound at every chosen mean-cost constraint**. For each cost cap, compute the best item-wise action assignment under that aggregate cap (with clearly stated access to test answers; analysis-only), and choose the best *fixed* action satisfying the same cap. The controller must never use test outcome tables at training/inference. Report gap closed only when the oracle/fixed/policy points are comparable, with uncertainty and undefined cases when the denominator is near zero.

## 2. Scientific question: can decoder loops substitute for visual tokens?

- **H1: allocation headroom.** Compare the constrained per-item oracle with the best single fixed action at matched cost, then the learned controller against fixed, rule-based and independently composed policies. This does not establish a new architecture; it tests whether per-item allocation on *the same weights* is worthwhile. *LoopZip* provides cross-call compression but not a 3×5 per-item budget–depth grid (PDF §§3–5 pp.2–8); external *SmartVL* already couples visual tokens with non-recurrent layer/head compute (§3.3, `Research_Gap_Analysis.md`). Do not claim “first” without a fresh literature check.
- **H2: interaction and task type.** For the same item, compare (low k, high R) with (high k, low R) at overlapping measured-cost bands, and fit an interaction model or item-cluster bootstrap. Predefine “perception-limited” and “reasoning-limited” from **independent** text-description and resolution-sensitivity probes on separate models, not from whether varloop's extra passes helped. Examine OCR, small text, charts, spatial grounding, and reasoning separately. Extra recurrent passes may improve interpretation but cannot recover pixels erased by downsampling; test this instead of asserting it. *RecursiveVLM* Table 6 PDF p.8 already shows depth need not help every task.
- **After Version B, not before:** If the grid shows a useful interaction and the cheap controller reliably captures it, consider Version C's question-conditioned *in-ViT* early dropping (which must save measured ViT latency) or on-policy GRPO and optional decoder LoRA. A loop-state-aware mid-inference controller is a distinct, more difficult January research extension; its additional observation-call, cache, and sequence-shape costs must be measured. Decoder LoRA changes the trainability claim. *Glance-ETD* PDF Fig.3/Table S15 pp.3–5,26, *LLaVA-UHD v4* pp.3–8, *GRIP-VLM* pp.8–12 and *TPRL* pp.4–13 are relevant controls.


## 3. Fair baselines and data

**Staged baselines:** for **December 3**, dense full-k at R=1–5; fixed k at native R=3; all fixed `(k,R)` points; k-only and R-only adaptive policies; random/uniform versus frozen text-guided selector; simple question-type/length heuristics; supervised cheapest-correct imitation; full-information cost-sensitive controller; and a hindsight oracle under **the same cost cap**. For **paper stage**, add *AdaptMerge* (PDF [007]), *StepPrune* ([059]), *LoopZip* ([038]) when implementation/backbone permits, *GRIP-VLM* ([053]) frozen-scorer, *TPRL* ([061]) frozen-LLM ablation and headline reported separately, and a SmartVL-style controller adapted fairly to the same backbone. Reimplementation of incompatible methods is not an automatic Dec 3 prerequisite. Match realized token counts, resolution/crops, answer length/decoding, and full-pipeline cost; do not compare published scores across checkpoints as if controlled. See `Methodology_Comparison.md`.

**Proposal's core evaluation:** GQA, TextVQA, ChartQA, MMMU and ScreenSpot-v2, with frozen item IDs and official scoring; dev selection separate from controller training and held-out testing. Other tasks (POPE, OCRBench, DocVQA, AI2D, MMStar) are optional paper-stage generalization checks. Audit image/question overlap with teacher masks and any selector development set. *AdaptMerge* Table 1 PDF p.6, *GRIP-VLM* Table 1 p.10, *Glance-ETD* Table 1 p.6, *StepPrune* Tables 1–4 pp.7–10 and *RecursiveVLM* Tables 1/6 pp.6,8 motivate task diversity; they do not validate the proposal's exact split sizes or scorer code.

**Measures:** official task metrics with paired uncertainty; action histogram `(k,R)` per input; realized tokens; ViT/FLOPs/latency, each loop prefill, generated-token latency, selector and thumbnail overhead, p50/p95 TTFT/response latency, peak memory, batch- and hardware-specific throughput. Plot per-task accuracy versus actual wall time **and** analytic FLOPs, not just k×R. For GUI injection, report clean task correctness, decoy rate, and attack success **conditional on clean correctness**; changing eligibility between cells can distort comparisons, so additionally report a paired reference-clean-correct cohort and its denominator. Safety-aware training is gated on measurement, not a Dec 3 headline. *LoopZip* Fig.8/Table 3 PDF pp.7–8 and *Glance-ETD* Table S15 p.26 demonstrate cost-accounting pitfalls.

## 4. Ablations that can actually falsify the hypothesis

1. **Question causality:** same screenshot/image with distinct questions, shuffled/blank question, image-only policy; K/R distribution and accuracy at equal realized cost. Separate effects of text-guided *selector* and text-conditioned *controller* (*Hierarchical Query-Aware Pruning* PDF pp.2–5; *StepPrune* Fig.3 pp.8–10).
2. **Cardinality vs identity and location:** fixed k with random/uniform/text-aware masks; learned k with a frozen selector; resolution scaling versus post-projector pruning, each at *realized* visual counts, including the full ViT and thumbnail costs. No learned in-ViT adapter is required in Version B. *MVP* Appendix Table 6 PDF pp.13–14; *Glance-ETD* Table S15 p.26.
3. **Loop/token interaction:** fixed 3×5 grid and constant-cost diagonals; controller restricted to k or R; independently composed versus joint 15-way controller; compare task types assigned without seeing the grid. *LoopZip* PDF Tables 2–3 pp.7–8; *RecursiveVLM* Table 6 p.8. A loop-state-conditioned selector is **not** a Version B ablation because the pre-decision controller cannot see that state.
4. **Learning method:** supervised cheapest-correct imitation versus exact full-table expected-reward training with identical features/data; GRPO only as an optional later experiment when an action's reward is not completely observed or jointly training backbone LoRA. Report optimizer/inference overhead separately. *GRIP-VLM* PDF §4 pp.8–11 is a later comparator, not justification to relabel full-information learning as RL.
5. **Safety measurement:** the same 15 cells for clean/attacked paired GUI items, harmful and matched benign typographic images. Report raw and capability-conditioned attack success, over-refusal, and a common reference-eligible stratum; decide on safety-aware training only after this measurement. *MM-SafetyBench* PDF §§3–4 pp.8–15; *FigStep* PDF §§5–7 pp.3–12. The proposal's cited loop-depth safety and token-pruning-safety papers were **not in the supplied PDF corpus** and require full primary-source reading before related-work claims.

## 5. Stop / continue criteria

- **Stop or re-scope** if TML varloop cannot load/execute across the supported R values on the available A40s, fails basic grounding, or the measured grid cannot be run within available wall time. Do not silently present an Ouro text-only sweep as a joint VLM result.
- **Do not require monotonic R gains.** A working checkpoint can peak at different passes by task. Stop the *controller* effort if the **constrained** oracle has little matched-cost headroom or the learned 15-action policy cannot beat fixed and cheap heuristic allocations; an additive K×R surface weakens H2, not the validity of the measurements.
- **Continue toward a paper** if the grid reveals a reproducible and non-circular, task-type-specific substitution effect, the controller improves the measured accuracy–cost frontier with uncertainty quantified, and the safety axis changes an actual deployment decision. The contribution may be empirical even if no trained vision encoder or loop-state-aware controller is built. Do not claim a first-in-field result without updating the outside-corpus search; January decoder LoRA must be reported separately from frozen-backbone Version B.
