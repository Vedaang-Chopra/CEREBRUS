# Research gap analysis — adversarial novelty assessment

**Evidence boundary (November 24, 2026):** “In corpus” means only the 77 supplied PDFs (74 distinct byte streams). “External check” refers to a targeted, non-exhaustive check of four additional primary research papers, cited as [1]–[4]. Absence from this set **cannot establish** field-wide novelty. Do not pitch the project as the first adaptive or question-conditioned visual compression method.

**Proposal received after the review:** Vedaang Chopra's *Adaptive Looped VLM — Research Vision & Implementation Blueprint* (November 22, 2026) is now preserved as `Project_Proposal_Reference.txt` and governs **Version B**: a frozen TML-d24-varloop-research backbone, existing text-guided token selector, full 3×5 `(k,R)` grid, and a controller trained from **all 15 known action outcomes per training item**. The proposed December 3 contribution is primarily an empirical token-versus-shared-loop substitution test, with a modest full-information controller and capability-conditioned safety measurement. It is **not** an on-policy GRPO, learned vision-encoder, or mid-loop state-aware policy at that stage. The proposal's claims and references outside this corpus (including RR-VLM, AdaVSkip, *Running Fewer Loops Removes Refusal*, *Token-Pruning-Induced Vulnerabilities*, and VPI-Bench) were **not** fully read or verified in this review; obtain those primary sources before using them to assert novelty.

| Query-aware token merging with variable output count, pretrained backbone unchanged | *AdaptMerge* threshold-selects and merges query-relevant tokens after encoder and image-diverse tokens inside vision pathway (§§3–4, pp.3–8). *Hierarchical Query-Aware Pruning* adjusts total K by image complexity and crop allocation by question (Eqs.1–8, PDF pp.2–5). | **Not novel** as a combination of query awareness, variable K, and a frozen decoder. | Trained frozen-decoder question-conditioned scorer | *MVP* trains question-cross-attending pruner while freezing ViT/projector/LLM (PDF §4 pp.4–8); *Glance-ETD* trains an evidence scorer with MLLM frozen (PDF §3 pp.3–7). | **Not novel.** MVP's thresholded K loses substantial TextVQA performance (Appendix Table 6, pp.13–14), leaving calibration as a practical problem. |
| Learned per-question visual-token count with frozen decoder | *StepPrune* learns a STOP action, optimizes answer CE plus expected K, and freezes original vision/connector/LLM (PDF §§3–4 pp.3–10). | **Already solved in a non-looped VLM setting.** The novelty bar requires more than replacing STOP with a different differentiable or RL head. |

## External primary-source checks that materially narrow the claim

- **SmartVL, *Look Less, Think Faster*** already jointly trains a visual-token controller and an LLM layer/head-compute controller under a sampled global FLOP budget; training includes answer loss and a differentiable budget estimator. Its §3.3 says it updates LLM, projector and controllers while freezing the *vision encoder*—the reverse of the proposed frozen-decoder constraint. It skips/prunes conventional decoder layers/heads, **not recurrent applications of a weight-shared decoder**. It is therefore a direct counterexample to any generic claim of “first joint visual-token and reasoning-compute optimizer,” although it does not settle the narrower loop-count/frozen-decoder version.[1]
- **LoopLook, *Loop to Look Better*** injects the question into repeated *vision-encoder* blocks, learns an exit gate, and optimizes answer CE plus expected visual recurrence depth with the language model frozen (§§3.2–3.5; §4.1). Thus “first question-conditioned adaptive recurrent vision-side training with a frozen decoder” is false. It does **not** claim a learned cardinality policy for visual tokens entering a *looped decoder*.[2]
- **AdaLoop, *Thinking with the Vision Encoder*** repeats question-conditioned vision layers with pretrained vision weights frozen, deeply supervises intermediate answers, and describes adaptive halting from answer stability (§§3.1–3.3). It further narrows claims around question-guided recurrent perception. Verify whether any particular proposed recurrent encoder mechanism is actually distinct before presenting it as new.[3]
- **LoopVL, *Recurrent Visual Intelligence*** supplies a real weight-shared, visual-token-bearing recurrent backbone and analyzes changed attention across calls; its default schedule and sweeps are not proof of an optimized per-input joint token/loop policy (§§2–4, §7). It is a candidate backbone **only after** checkpoint/code/hardware and modifiable runtime are actually validated.[4]

The supplied `2026_TML-d24-varloop-research-model-card.txt` is also important non-PDF context: it states a November 20, 2026 release, open weights, 1–5 supported shared-middle-block passes and ~20 GB checkpoint with at least 32 GB inference memory. This is **provider documentation, not our reproduction**. It may be a cleaner variable-loop testbed than imposing unsupported halting on another checkpoint.

## What might still be defensible — hypotheses, not established contributions

1. **Version B's controlled 3×5 exchange-rate measurement:** on **one set of pretrained variable-loop weights**, measure each item under k={25%,50%,100%} visual retention and R=1–5 true shared-decoder passes, with full-pipeline cost and a consistent selector. The specific empirical substitution effect must be demonstrated, not declared from architecture. Compare against *LoopZip* (looped compression, PDF §§3–5 pp.2–8), *StepPrune* (non-looped learned per-input k, pp.3–10), and the external *SmartVL* (non-recurrent joint token and depth/width controller, §3.3).[1]
2. **A cheap *pre-inference* joint `(k,R)` controller:** use cached question/thumbnail features and complete training-grid reward vectors; compare full-information cost-sensitive learning with cheapest-correct imitation, best fixed action, k-only, R-only, and simple heuristics at equal measured mean cost. This is a modest application, **not a new RL algorithm** or a loop-state-aware controller. The oracle must be recomputed under each aggregate cost cap: “cheapest correct per item” alone is not the upper bound at arbitrary cost. The test outcome grid is analysis-only and must never leak into controller training.
3. **A non-circular H2 and measured safety axis:** define perception/reasoning-limited strata with independent probe models *before* inspecting varloop grid outcomes; test whether low-k/high-R wins on reasoning items and loses on perception items at comparable realized cost. For GUI injection, report clean target success, decoy selection and attack success conditional on clean capability, plus a common reference-eligible cohort; varying denominators can falsely suggest safety. This is hypothesis-driven empirical work, not an established positive finding. *MM-SafetyBench* PDF §§3–4 pp.8–15 and *FigStep* PDF §§5–7 pp.3–12 are supplied background; the loop-depth and token-pruning safety papers named in the proposal remain outside-corpus unverified inputs.

**Later Version C / January only:** question-conditioned early-ViT dropping, on-policy GRPO, a mid-loop state-aware policy, or decoder LoRA can be considered if Version B clears its gates. Decoder LoRA does **not** satisfy the frozen-decoder condition; it must be reported separately. A mere composition of InstructBLIP question queries + StepPrune STOP + GRIP-VLM's GRPO reward + a recurrent backbone remains incremental unless a loop-specific mechanism, a stronger matched-cost Pareto frontier, or a well-isolated empirical finding emerges.

## Required falsification tests before pitching a paper

- **Causal per-question budget:** same image/different questions, shuffled question, image-only router, and K-conditioned scorers at exactly matched test-set mean and bucketed K. *StepPrune* PDF Fig.3 pp.8–10 and *Hierarchical Query-Aware Pruning* PDF Eqs.1–8 pp.2–5 motivate these controls.
- **True loop interaction:** fixed-K × fixed-loop grid; k-only; R-only; independently composed policies; joint *pre-inference* 15-way policy; LoopZip's cross-call selection as a paper-stage baseline where compatible; same realized cost cap. Report differences by independently assigned perception/reasoning strata; **do not** claim K changes conditional on observed recurrent state in Version B. That requires a separate mid-loop policy. *LoopZip* PDF Tables 2–3 pp.7–8, *RecursiveVLM* Table 6 p.8.
- **Frozen boundary:** Version B uses cached outcomes and trains only the pre-inference controller—no backbone gradient is required. Verify pretrained weights unchanged. For Version C, list every trainable group and verify gradients can reach its visual module through a frozen decoder; for January decoder LoRA, explicitly label the condition **non-frozen**. *StepPrune* PDF pp.3–6 and *GRIP-VLM* PDF pp.8–10 are frozen-backbone comparators.
- **End-to-end accounting:** include initial ViT, language prefill on every decoder call, controller, multiple visual re-encodes, teacher/correction overhead, generation, peak activation/weight memory, and wall-clock latency on specified hardware. Report mean, high percentile, and task-stratified Pareto curves, not only retained tokens or attention FLOPs. *LoopZip* PDF Fig.8 and Table 3 pp.7–8; *Glance-ETD* Table S15 p.26.
- **Reproducibility/overlap:** separate training/eval images and questions, check contamination when using GQA/TextVQA/OCRBench or teacher-derived masks, repeat seeds, report real released checkpoints and per-stage GPU hours. *MVP* PDF §4 pp.4–9 and Appendix Table 8; *AdaptVision* PDF Appendix A.2.
